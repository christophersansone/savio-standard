# The Convictions of Our Catholic School Community

*The Savio Standard · One page — for parents, teachers, staff, and leadership to sign together; see also the [parent version](convictions-parents.md)*

> _"In everything, do to others what you would have them do to you, for this sums up the Law and the Prophets."_
> **— Jesus Christ (Matthew 7:12)**

Christ gave this command to His disciples, and through them to His Church, as the ordinary standard of Christian life. We believe a Catholic school community should be where that standard is most visible, not only in what is taught, but in how parents, students, teachers, staff, and leaders treat one another every day.

We, the parents, teachers, staff, and leadership of **[School]**, choose this community on purpose, and we choose it anew every day. The parents among us entrust their children to this community; the rest of us receive that trust as sacred. The convictions that follow are our shared understanding of what it means to live Christ's command together.

- Every child is made in the image of God and bears a dignity that no one's behavior, position, or convenience may diminish.
- **The Golden Rule is the standard of a Christian community**, not merely its aspiration.
- Parents are the first educators of their children, and the school is their indispensable partner; neither can form a child alone.
- A Catholic school is more than an academic institution: it is the community where children spend their days learning how to live with one another and with the adults entrusted to guide them.
- The Faith is not just a subject. It is _taught_ in religion class and _lived_ in the hallway, the lunchroom, the faculty room, the sports field, and the group chat.

Therefore, in how we live together, **we choose:**

- **The dignity of every child** over the comfort of any adult.
- **One standard for every family** over exceptions for any family, regardless of generosity, employment, influence, or the difficulty of a conversation.
- **Formation** over mere discipline, and the kind of discipline necessary whenever formation requires it.
- **Discernment** over false balance: when accounts conflict, we consider patterns, context, power dynamics, and the courage it often takes to speak up, knowing that treating unequal things equally is not justice.
- **Truth spoken with charity** over peace preserved through silence.
- **The protection of the vulnerable** over the preservation of institutional reputation.
- **Partnership between home and school** over control of one by the other.
- **Repair and restoration** over punishment alone, and over pretending that harm has not occurred.

That is: while the things on the right have real value, whenever they conflict with the things on the left, we choose the things on the left. Every time.

**As parents,** we will model this standard at home, believe our children capable of both goodness and harm, receive difficult news with humility and gratitude, bring concerns directly and charitably, and support the school's judgments even when those judgments concern our own child.

**As teachers and staff,** we will recognize how children treat one another as part of their Catholic formation, notice the child who is excluded, act with courage when we see harm, and never allow a family's standing to determine what we are willing to see.

**As administrators,** we will apply one standard to every child without favoritism, exercise discernment rather than false balance, address aggression firmly enough to change behavior, protect the child who speaks up, and treat parents as true partners: informed early, involved early, and trusted with the truth.

**As pastor and parish leadership,** we will proclaim this standard wherever we are heard, hold the whole community — including the school — accountable to it, and shepherd every family in it, especially those who are struggling most.

We make these commitments not because they are easy, but because we believe they are true. When this community lives according to the standard it professes, we will be its strongest advocates, serving together for the good of every child entrusted to our care.
