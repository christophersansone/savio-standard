# The Convictions of Catholic School Parents

*The Savio Standard · One page — the parent version; see also the [community version](convictions-community.md)*

We are parents who choose a Catholic school on purpose—not once, but every day—entrusting to it what is most precious to us: our children. We do so because we believe that Catholic education is more than academic excellence: it is the formation of young people in truth, virtue, and communion with Christ.

We know that no school community is perfect because no community is made of perfect people. Children will fail, parents will fail, and schools will sometimes fail. Our hope is not for perfection, but for a community that responds to failure with truth, charity, justice, and the courage to begin again.

We believe these truths are shared by all who seek to follow Christ:

- Every child is made in the image of God and bears a dignity that no other person's behavior or opinion may diminish.
- **The Golden Rule is the standard of a Christian community**, not merely its aspiration.
- Parents are the first and primary educators of their children; the school is our indispensable partner, never our replacement.
- A Catholic school is more than an academic institution: it is the community where our children spend their days learning how to live with one another and with the adults entrusted to guide them.
- The Faith is not just a subject. It is _taught_ in religion class and _lived_ in the hallway, the lunchroom, the sports field, and the group chat.

Because we believe these truths, we believe they should be evident in the life of every Catholic school community. We therefore state our expectations plainly, beginning with ourselves.

**We expect of our children:**

- That they treat every classmate with kindness and dignity.
- That they include the excluded.
- That they refuse to be an audience for cruelty, speaking up or seeking help when someone is mistreated.
- That when they cause harm, they acknowledge it honestly and work to repair it.

**We expect of ourselves:**

- That we model at home and in the community what we expect in the classroom.
- That we believe our own child capable of both great goodness and genuine wrongdoing.
- That we bring our concerns directly, privately, and charitably, following Christ's instruction in Matthew 18 rather than the bleachers or the group chat.
- That when another parent brings us difficult news about our child, we receive it with humility rather than defensiveness, seeking what is true and working together toward restoration.
- That we show up for this community with our _presence_, not only our tuition or tithe.

**We expect of our fellow parents:**

- No more and no less than we expect of ourselves.

**We therefore expect of our school:**

- **That the Faith be evident not only in the curriculum but throughout the life of the school:** how children treat one another is an essential part of Catholic formation, not an interruption to it.
- **That one standard apply to every child, without favoritism:** every family should be able to trust that expectations are applied fairly and consistently.
- **That when accounts conflict, prudent discernment prevail:** considering patterns, context, power dynamics, and the courage it often takes for a child to speak up, remembering that justice requires more than treating every situation as though it were the same.
- **That aggression be addressed firmly and consistently enough to change behavior,** protecting those who are harmed while helping those who cause harm grow in virtue, responsibility, and reconciliation.
- **That parents be treated as true partners:** communicating early, involving us early, and trusting us with the truth.

When our school lives out these convictions, we will be its strongest advocates: defending it publicly, supporting its judgments even when they concern our own child, assuming goodwill in difficult decisions, and working alongside its leaders for the good of every student.

These convictions hold us accountable first. We offer them not as demands placed upon others, but as the standard by which we ourselves desire to be judged. If we live them together—as parents, educators, and students—we believe our schools can become communities where the Gospel is not only taught, but unmistakably lived.
